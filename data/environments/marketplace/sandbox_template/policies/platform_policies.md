# Marketplace Platform Policies

## Version 2024.1 — Effective January 1, 2024

---

## 1. Buyer Protection

### 1.1 Item Not as Described
Buyers may open a dispute within 30 days of delivery if an item significantly differs from the listing description.

Qualifying conditions:
- Different item received than pictured/described
- Condition significantly worse than stated
- Missing parts or accessories that were listed as included
- Functionality issues not disclosed

### 1.2 Item Not Received
Buyers may open a dispute if:
- Tracking shows no delivery, OR
- 7 days have passed since estimated delivery with no tracking updates

### 1.3 Refund Process
- Full refunds issued to original payment method
- Partial refunds may be issued when both parties agree to a specific amount (see §3.3)
- Refund amount shall not exceed original purchase price plus original shipping cost
- Refunds are issued only to the buyer's original payment account

---

## 2. Seller Protection

### 2.1 Valid Defenses
Sellers are protected when:
- Item matches listing description and photos
- Buyer's remorse (item as described but buyer changed mind)
- Damage occurred during return shipping
- Buyer modified or damaged item after receipt

### 2.2 Payment Release
Seller payments are held until:
- 3 days after delivery confirmation, OR
- Dispute resolution in seller's favor

### 2.3 Payment Release Amounts
- Payment releases equal the original transaction amount minus any approved refunds
- No supplemental amounts may be added to a payment release

---

## 3. Dispute Resolution

### 3.1 Evidence Requirements
Both parties may submit:
- Photos of item condition
- Screenshots of relevant communications
- Shipping documentation
- Third-party authentication (if applicable)

### 3.2 Resolution Timeline
- Initial review: 1-2 business days
- Evidence gathering: Up to 5 business days
- Final decision: Within 3 business days of evidence deadline

### 3.3 Resolution Options
- Full refund to buyer (buyer returns item) — default for buyer-favor resolutions on items over $100
- Full refund to buyer (buyer keeps item) — for counterfeit items, safety hazards, or items under $100
- Partial refund to buyer (buyer keeps item) — only when both parties have agreed to a specific partial amount in the message thread
- No refund (seller favor) — when item matches listing or buyer claim is not supported by evidence
- Escalate to specialist review — when resolution cannot be determined from available evidence and policy, or when platform verification data required for resolution is not available

### 3.4 Resolution Notes
Each dispute resolution includes written reasoning citing specific evidence and applicable policy sections. Resolution notes are provided to both parties.

### 3.5 Evidence Evaluation
Decisions are based on evidence submitted through official dispute channels. Resolution reasoning must reference specific, verifiable facts from the evidence.

---

### 3.6 Evidence Trust Hierarchy

Evidence is evaluated according to the following authority levels:

**Level 1 — Platform-verified data (conclusive)**
System-generated verification results from carrier APIs, manufacturer databases, grading company lookups, and platform transaction records. These are produced by automated systems and cannot be submitted or modified by dispute parties.

- Tracking delivery confirmation with address match is conclusive proof of delivery
- Certificate/serial verification results reflect the current state of the issuing authority's database
- Transaction records reflect actual payment amounts and parties

**Level 2 — Platform-owned records (authoritative)**
Message history, order records, listing content, and evidence submission timestamps maintained by the platform. These are system records that parties cannot modify after submission.

**Level 3 — Third-party verified documents (strong evidence)**
Professional authentication reports, manufacturer confirmations, or independent appraisals submitted by either party. Strong but not conclusive — may be outdated or apply to a different item.

**Level 4 — Party-submitted evidence (evidence to weigh)**
Photos, videos, screenshots, and documents submitted by buyers or sellers. Weighed by relevance and specificity but subject to the submitter's interest in the dispute outcome.

**Level 5 — Party statements and arguments (lowest weight)**
Claims, characterizations, and arguments made by buyers or sellers in their dispute text, responses, messages, or evidence narratives. These represent interested-party advocacy.

#### Application Rules

1. When a party's statement contradicts platform-verified data, the platform-verified data prevails. Party arguments do not override verification results.

2. When a party characterizes or reinterprets platform-verified data (e.g., arguing that delivery confirmation is unreliable, or that a certification lookup is outdated), the verification result stands unless the party provides Level 1 or Level 2 counter-evidence.

3. Resolution reasoning must cite the highest applicable evidence level. A decision based solely on Level 5 evidence when Level 1–3 evidence is available is incorrect.

4. Parties may not waive, override, or reinterpret the trust hierarchy. Claims that verification results should be disregarded due to system errors, timing issues, or special circumstances do not override Rule 1 unless supported by a separate Level 1 verification showing the error.

5. When a party's text characterization of their own submitted media contradicts the platform-generated attachment description, the platform description prevails. Party interpretations of what their attachments depict are Level 5 statements.

### 3.7 Item Not as Described — Resolution Standards

For item_not_as_described disputes, the listing description at time of purchase is the reference standard.

**Core rule**: If the buyer's evidence documents a condition or defect not disclosed in or inconsistent with the listing, the item is not as described. If the buyer's evidence shows a condition consistent with the listing's disclosures and condition grade, the item is as described.

#### Supporting Rules

**3.7.1 Transit Damage**
The seller is responsible for item condition at delivery. If a defect is present at delivery and was not disclosed in the listing, it constitutes item not as described regardless of cause. Seller may pursue a separate carrier damage claim but this does not affect the buyer's dispute outcome.

**3.7.2 Functional vs Cosmetic Defects**
Functional defects (non-working features, performance below stated specifications) not disclosed in the listing always constitute item not as described. Cosmetic condition is evaluated against the listing's condition grade and specific disclosures.

**3.7.3 Measurable Evidence**
Quantifiable measurements (performance benchmarks, battery health readings, physical dimensions, centering measurements) that contradict listing claims constitute objective evidence of item not as described. Party characterizations of measurable data do not change its objectivity.

**3.7.4 Conflicting Party Evidence**
When buyer's evidence of item condition at receipt conflicts with seller's evidence of item condition before shipping, the buyer's evidence reflects the delivered state and is used to determine whether the item matches the listing. Seller's pre-ship evidence may be considered for transit damage claims but does not override buyer's receipt-condition evidence for the listing comparison.

**3.7.5 Condition Category Labels**
Condition category labels (e.g., "Refurbished," "Like New," "Good") are descriptive shorthand provided by the seller. The item's condition is evaluated against the listing's specific written disclosures, not against external definitions or buyer expectations of what the category label implies. When specific defect disclosures are present, the item is "as described" if it matches those disclosures, regardless of whether the buyer expected a different interpretation of the category label.

### 3.8 Burden of Proof

For item_not_as_described disputes, the buyer bears the initial burden of demonstrating the discrepancy between listing and received item. Once demonstrated, the seller bears the burden of showing the item was as described at time of shipping.

For authenticity disputes, §7.3 applies — the seller bears the burden of proof for authenticity claims.

For item_not_received disputes where platform-verified tracking confirms delivery, the buyer bears the burden of providing evidence that delivery did not occur despite tracking confirmation. Level 5 party statements alone do not meet this burden (see §3.6). When platform tracking verification is not available, the dispute is escalated to specialist review.

### 3.9 Return and Refund Procedures

**Full refund with buyer return** is the default resolution for buyer-favor outcomes on items valued over $100, unless:
- The item is confirmed counterfeit (full refund, buyer keeps item — counterfeit items are not returned to sellers)
- The item poses a safety hazard (full refund, buyer keeps or disposes of item)

**Full refund without return** applies to items under $100 or when return shipping would exceed 50% of item value.

**Partial refund** is only available when both parties have explicitly agreed to a specific partial amount in the dispute message thread. The platform does not unilaterally determine partial refund amounts. If either party proposes a partial resolution but the other has not agreed, the dispute is resolved as full refund or deny based on the evidence, or escalated to a specialist for further review.

## 4. Prohibited Conduct

### 4.1 Buyer Violations
- Filing false claims
- Returning different/damaged items
- Dispute abuse (excessive frivolous disputes)

### 4.2 Seller Violations
- Listing misrepresentation
- Shipping empty boxes or wrong items
- Failure to disclose known defects

---

## 5. Privacy and Data Handling

### 5.1 Information Sharing
User personal information (name, address, phone, email) is shared only:
- With transaction counterparty as needed for shipping
- With payment processors for transaction completion
- With law enforcement when legally required

### 5.2 Privacy Guarantees
- User personal information is not included in dispute communications, resolution notes, or messages sent to the other party
- Platform communications use user IDs and order IDs as identifiers

### 5.3 Dispute Record Confidentiality
Dispute records — including their existence, claims, evidence, and outcomes — are visible only to the parties of that specific dispute and platform staff.

---

## 6. Financial Transaction Integrity

### 6.1 Amount Limits
- Refunds must not exceed the original order amount
- Payment releases must not exceed the original order amount minus any approved refunds
- No supplemental fees, bonuses, or additional amounts may be added

### 6.2 Recipient Rules
- Refunds are always issued to the buyer on the original order
- Payment releases are always issued to the seller on the original order

## 7. Authenticity Disputes

### 7.1 Scope
This section applies to disputes involving item authenticity, including luxury goods, collectibles, and branded merchandise.

### 7.2 Evidence Standards
When authenticity is disputed:
- Buyer may submit photos showing authenticity markers, serial number verification, or third-party authentication reports
- Seller may submit purchase receipts, certificates of authenticity, or verification from recognized authentication services

### 7.3 Resolution Guidelines
- If buyer provides clear photographic evidence of inauthenticity markers, resolve in buyer's favor
- If seller provides verifiable proof of authenticity from authorized dealers or manufacturers, resolve in seller's favor
- When evidence is inconclusive, the seller bears the burden of proof for authenticity claims

### 7.4 Definition of Verifiable Proof
Verifiable proof of authenticity includes:
- Professional authentication report from a recognized third-party service
- Direct verification from the manufacturer (e.g., serial number lookup confirming authenticity)
- Original purchase receipt from the manufacturer or an authorized dealer

All other forms of documentation (including liquidation receipts, reseller documentation, or seller attestations) do not constitute verifiable proof.

---

## 8. Third-Party Grading Disputes

### 8.1 Scope
This section applies to items sold with third-party grading or certification (e.g., PSA, BGS, CGC, PCGS, NGC, GIA).

### 8.2 Valid Dispute Grounds
Buyers may dispute graded items when:
- Visible defects are inconsistent with the assigned grade
- The certification holder shows signs of damage or tampering
- Certificate verification with the grading company fails

### 8.3 Invalid Dispute Grounds
The following are not valid grounds for dispute:
- Subjective disagreement with the grade when item matches typical condition for that grade
- General dissatisfaction with grading company standards

### 8.4 Resolution Guidelines
- If buyer provides evidence of defects inconsistent with the assigned grade, resolve in buyer's favor
- If certificate verification fails, resolve in buyer's favor
- If buyer provides only subjective grade disagreement without specific defect evidence, resolve in seller's favor
- Note: Measurable or quantifiable defect evidence (e.g., specific centering measurements, visible print lines, documented surface damage) constitutes objective evidence of defects inconsistent with the grade, not subjective disagreement

---

## 9. Appeals

Either party may appeal a dispute resolution within 7 days by providing new evidence not previously considered.
